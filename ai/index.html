AI in My Workflow

Localization, editorial planning, video, repurposing, GEO and AEO, and the tools I build with Claude Code

I'm a digital marketer, and AI is part of how I work every day. I use it for the slow, repetitive parts so I have more time for the ideas, and I check everything it makes before it goes out. These are the places it shows up, from posts localized for 10+ regions to apps I built for myself.

One Post, Ready for 10+ Regions

At Cohesity I built an AI agent with Claude Code, loaded it with our style guide and brand guidelines, and built a localization feature into the Champions Hub. Employees browsed posts in their own language and region and shared them to LinkedIn.

The agent adapted the region as much as the language: less US-centric, with the tone set for each place. Its versions came out about 99% ready. I reviewed them with my team and select Champion advocates, and every fix went back to the agent so it learned.

See the 3D version on the Cohesity page

From Calendar to Do's and Don'ts

At QAD Redzone and Cohesity I used AI across the editorial workflow: building the content calendar in Monday.com, writing briefs and first drafts that I then edited, setting up A/B tests, localizing content, and turning our guidelines into do's and don'ts docs the whole team could follow.

93 Seconds of Takes, Cut to 43

The Mars Men PSA was a project for a job interview. I had six takes: two hooks, three body takes and a call to action. AI transcribed every take, so I could read the shoot instead of scrubbing through it, and it helped me pick the hook. Then I cut the video by editing the transcript, and AI made the captions for sound-off viewing.

The short hook won. The "You weren't built to be average" take moved up to sit right behind it, and lines that repeated a point came out.

One Episode, Five Pieces

A demonstration on a public episode of Cohesity's Resilience Universe podcast: CEO Sanjay Poonen with Kevin Mandia, 26 minutes. AI reads the transcript and marks the moments that stand on their own. Each one is then cut for where it will run, in the shape and length that place expects.

Written for Search Engines and AI Answers

People type keywords into Google. They ask ChatGPT a question. GEO and AEO, generative and answer engine optimization, mean writing for both.

At QAD Redzone in 2024 I organized our FAQs and blogs so search engines and AI answers could find them. At Nike I wrote alt text and product tags for both kinds of search: for someone who knows exactly what they want, and for someone who only knows roughly.
