How I Shipped What 20 Million Nike Customers See First

Digital Content Publishing for Nike's App and Nike.com | Contract via Insight Global

Nike's app and website form one of the largest direct-to-consumer retail surfaces in the world. Each featured placement, landing page, product grid, and notification started as a decision someone made upstream, then had to be executed correctly in front of an enormous audience. For three months in 2026, that execution was my job.

THE PROBLEM

Content goes out continuously on both the app and the site, reaching people within minutes of going live. Requests arrive from marketing partners across the business faster than one calendar absorbs them, and a single campaign can touch an app shopping surface, a website landing page, a product grid, a notification, and the routing that connects all of it.

Some requests arrive incomplete: assets missing, timing that collides with work already scheduled, asks the platform cannot support. Delivery decides whether a campaign reaches anyone at all.

THE SOLUTION

Each execution stayed with me from request through confirmation, and the check happened on a real device before anything got scheduled, since a desktop preview hides what a phone shows.

Conflicts and platform limits went back to partners while there was still time to change the plan. After each send, delivery data confirmed the content reached people the way the brief intended. Partners handed me a request and got back a live, working execution on the date they needed it.

Two smaller pieces of the job came out of the same seat: headline, product, and entry-point tests on live placements, reported in wrap briefs, and the team's authoring reference, which I drafted as the onboarding guide for incoming publishers.

Building the Placements People See First

A featured placement is often the first thing someone sees when they open Nike.com or the app. It has to load instantly, read in a glance, and hold up with the sound off, which is how most people meet it.

Mine ran from request to live: sourcing the imagery and copy, constructing the placement, previewing it on device, and routing it into campaigns including the U.S. Open Tennis Championships, Back to School, and the FIFA World Cup, where updates published against live match results.

Athlete Stories, Published on a Seasonal Beat

Some of the highest-stakes placements were built around real athletes. For Back to School, featured placements on incoming UCLA midfielder Mia Corona and USC tight end Mark Bowman paired their photography with copy and routed each into the gear from the shoot.

Talent-led content carries a different weight than product content. The names, the imagery, and the timing all have to be exactly right, and the season sets the publishing window. Getting these live correctly, on the day they were meant to run, was the whole job.

Full Shopping Surfaces

Beyond single placements, I built complete shopping experiences: the hero, the category navigation, the product grid, and the routing that connects them.

The August Promotional Shop shows the assembly involved. Six category entry points, each with its own imagery and menu routing, linked into the parent surface alongside the featured carousel and product grids. Components were built one at a time and assembled in sequence, and the surface only worked once each piece resolved to the right destination.

A tile that lands nowhere is a product someone cannot buy.
